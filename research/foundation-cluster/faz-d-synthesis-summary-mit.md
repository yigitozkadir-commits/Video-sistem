## Evidence & Confidence Summary

### Pre-Islamic Evidence (Primary Sources: Orkhon Inscriptions)

**C-MIT-001: Kayra/Tengri as Sky Deity**
- **Confidence:** ESTABLISHED (primary source unambiguous)
- **Evidence:** Orkhon inscriptions (8th century) reference Tengri for sovereignty/victory
- **Production note:** Strong foundation; no revision needed

**C-MIT-004: Umay Goddess**
- **Confidence:** ESTABLISHED (multiple independent attestations)
- **Evidence:** Orkhon inscriptions + Altai epics + Mongolian shamanism
- **Etymology:** Old Turkic "umāy" (placenta/afterbirth) confirmed
- **Caveat:** Linguistic continuity ≠ belief continuity (names survive after practices fade)
- **Production note:** Straightforward; note name persistence as linguistic evidence, not behavioral evidence

**C-MIT-008: Minimalist Orkhon Theology**
- **Confidence:** ESTABLISHED (primary reading transparent)
- **Evidence:** Orkhon inscriptions depict Tengri as distant deity, invoked for sovereignty, no elaborate ritual documented
- **Critical importance:** This is baseline for evaluating modern reconstructions
- **Production note:** No revision; essential context for authenticity debate

### Post-Orkhon/Secondary Claims

**C-MIT-002: Ülgen (Good Deity)**
- **Confidence:** PROBABLE (limited primary source evidence)
- **Faz C red-flag:** SOURCE_QUALITY_CONCERN — Secondary sources may reflect later Mongolian syncretism
- **Production note:** Downgrade to PROBABLE; mark as possibly syncretic tradition

**C-MIT-003: Erlik (Evil/Underworld)**
- **Confidence:** STRONGLY_SUPPORTED (documented across Altaic traditions)
- **Caveat:** Dualism shared across Central Asian/Siberian religions (not uniquely Turkic)
- **Production note:** Confirm dualism is Altaic pattern, not Turkic innovation

**C-MIT-005: Pre-Islamic Sacrifice**
- **Confidence:** PLAUSIBLE (speculative; inferred from ethnographic parallel)
- **Faz C red-flag:** SPECULATION_RISK — Archaeological evidence limited
- **Production note:** Mark as hypothesis, not documented fact

### Modern Tengriism

**C-MIT-007: Modern Tengriism Movement**
- **Confidence:** ESTABLISHED (contemporary phenomenon well-documented)
- **Evidence:** Early 21st century (Burhanettin Mumcuoğlu 1999 official registration)
- **Caveat:** Modern movement ≠ unbroken transmission from pre-Islamic period
- **Production note:** Clear temporal/conceptual separation from pre-Islamic sources

**C-MIT-009: Gap between Orkhon & Modern Reconstruction**
- **Confidence:** STRONGLY_SUPPORTED (source gap is real)
- **Issue:** Orkhon minimalist; modern elaboration (rituals, doctrines) absent from primary sources
- **Causation problem:** Gap could reflect (a) oral traditions unwritten, (b) later development, (c) Islamic influence, (d) modern additions
- **No way to adjudicate** without new primary sources
- **Production note:** Acknowledge gap explicitly; frame as ongoing historiographic debate

**C-MIT-010: Shnirelman Critique ("Invention of Tradition")**
- **Confidence:** CONTESTED (valid scholarly concern, but modern Tengriists offer counter-arguments)
- **Faz C finding:** HISTORIOGRAPHY_DEBATE — "Invention of tradition" is analytical category, not empirical fact
- **Motivation question:** Complex; cannot assume nationalist agenda alone
- **Production note:** Frame as legitimate scholarly debate; neither dismiss nor accept as settled

**C-MIT-011: Borrowed Symbolic Motifs**
- **Confidence:** PLAUSIBLE (requires concrete examples)
- **Status:** REQUIRES_EVIDENCE — currently unsupported
- **Production note:** Do not include without specific exemplification
