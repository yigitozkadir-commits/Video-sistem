## Executive Summary for Production

**Core Narrative Arc** (450s / ~1240 words @ 165 wpm):

Ecel Perçemi represents a three-thousand-year continuity in Turkish warrior spirituality. Beginning as a Hunnic childhood protection ritual (Aydar), evolving through Göktürk philosophy (Kut = divine force), and finally synthesized with Islamic theology (Ecel = Allah's predetermined death time), the practice culminated in the Ottoman Serdengeçti—elite volunteer assault units who shaved their heads completely except for a single tuft, making themselves living symbols of acceptance toward death.

The perçem (top-tuft) functioned simultaneously as:
1. **Practical**: hygiene (lice prevention), armor padding (helmet protection)
2. **Ethical**: corpse dignity (enemy holds perçem, not face)
3. **Mystical**: angel conduit (divine beings lift soul by hair)

This synthesis transformed pre-Islamic warrior ethos into Islamic framework without losing spiritual continuity. The 450-second video traces this evolution across five narrative sections, ending with the perçem's echo in Ottoman literary tradition (Dede Korkut epics, Divan poetry's zülüf/lovelock symbolism).
